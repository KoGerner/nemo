Franziska Dohm: Thank you for the time, Henning. First the process itself: what does it do, and where does it sit?

Henning Barsch: The process covers taking back and disposing of food from the retail locations that was complained about or left over. The retail locations prepare the food in special boxes, and the staff take them along in the course of a delivery. At the production and processing sites these boxes are disposed of in the containers provided for them. It is a core process, and its process owner is plant management. It has no upstream and no downstream process.

Henning Barsch: Health of persons. No impact from a standstill. In words: 1 h –, 2 h none, 4 h none, 8 h none, 24 h none, 72 h none, 168 h none.

Henning Barsch: Impact health: 1 h –, 2 h 1, 4 h 1, 8 h 1, 24 h 1, 72 h 1, 168 h 1.

Franziska Dohm: How high is the potential threat, and what do you set for recovery?

Henning Barsch: The potential threat is high. Returns and animal by-product disposal has an RTO (recovery time objective) of 72 hours, because a standstill of the process beyond that time has considerable operational impacts. If the disposed food is not collected within 72 hours, this can interrupt the whole disposal process. That could lead to undisposed food piling up in the retail locations and at the production and processing sites, which in turn would badly impair operations.

Henning Barsch: RTO: 72 h.

Henning Barsch: MTPD: >168 h.

Henning Barsch: The process is relevant to the BCMS.

Franziska Dohm: What could make the process fail?

Henning Barsch: Failure of the disposal partner; missing or unusable disposal containers; failure of the fleet or of the drivers; exhausted intermediate storage capacity; access or transport disruption at operating sites.

Franziska Dohm: And how do you run it in emergency operation?

Henning Barsch: Separate, hygienically safe intermediate storage and the documentation of returns are kept up. Ten fleet staff take over prioritised collections. If the regular disposal partner fails, an alternative approved service provider is activated. Container and storage capacities are steered across sites. Collections are prioritised by fill level, hygiene risk and available storage time.

Franziska Dohm: Last, the resources the process needs.

Henning Barsch: Technical resources. Vehicle fleet: Delivery is made with our own fleet, which consists of about 60 internal lorries across all sites. Workaround: Resources from the central logistics fleet and from external carriers are possible at short notice.

Henning Barsch: Roles. Fleet staff: 15 in normal operation, 10 in emergency operation, needed within 72 h, on site. The job covers loading and delivering raw materials and meat products. In normal operation this takes 15 people. Workaround: In emergency operation 10 people are needed. The staff pool is sufficient. Additional staff can be requested from warehouse and transport.

Henning Barsch: Partners. Disposal contractor: Müllnix GmbH is responsible for collecting and maintaining the disposal containers. Workaround: Other external service providers are available on the market. A short-term switch is possible.

Henning Barsch: Depends on: Vehicle fleet, Fleet staff.
