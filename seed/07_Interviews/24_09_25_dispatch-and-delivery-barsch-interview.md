Franziska Dohm: Thank you for the time, Henning. First the process itself: what does it do, and where does it sit?

Henning Barsch: Goods dispatch and delivery begins with loading the goods onto the lorry. Delivery then follows with the company's own fleet to the various retail locations or warehouse sites. It is a core process, and its process owner is plant management. Its upstream process is order picking; it has no downstream process.

Henning Barsch: Impact financial: 1 h –, 2 h 1, 4 h 1, 8 h 1, 24 h 1, 72 h 1, 168 h 1.

Henning Barsch: Legal. There are no SLAs within the group. There are no defined quantities that have to be delivered either. In words: 1 h –, 2 h none, 4 h none, 8 h none, 24 h none, 72 h none, 168 h none.

Henning Barsch: Impact operational: 1 h –, 2 h 1, 4 h 1, 8 h 1, 24 h 1, 72 h 2, 168 h 4.

Franziska Dohm: How high is the potential threat, and what do you set for recovery?

Henning Barsch: The potential threat is high. The RTO (recovery time objective) of 72 hours for goods dispatch and delivery was set because up to that point deliveries can be made later. After that, high yield problems are to be expected. Note that the set-up times are limited. A workaround through transport and logistics or through external forwarders is possible. A standstill of this process has no immediate negative impact on the health of persons, and causes no financial losses. There are no legal obligations or SLAs (service level agreements) within the group either that would demand a faster recovery.

Henning Barsch: RTO: 72 h.

Henning Barsch: MTPD: >168 h.

Henning Barsch: The process is relevant to the BCMS.

Franziska Dohm: What could make the process fail?

Henning Barsch: Failure or unavailability of the fleet; staff shortage among drivers and loading staff; disruption of the traffic or transport infrastructure; failure of a logistics partner or of a workshop; interruption of the cold chain; missing dispatch or route data.

Franziska Dohm: And how do you run it in emergency operation?

Henning Barsch: Routes are prioritised by the availability of goods, by how perishable they are and by how much the supply depends on them. Ten fleet staff take over loading and delivery. Plant management takes over operational leadership. Missing internal vehicles are replaced by the central logistics fleet or by external carriers. Routes without priority are bundled, postponed or delivered later.

Franziska Dohm: Last, the resources the process needs.

Henning Barsch: Technical resources. Vehicle fleet: Delivery is made with our own fleet, which consists of about 60 internal lorries across all sites. Workaround: Resources from the central logistics fleet and from external carriers are possible at short notice.

Henning Barsch: Roles. Fleet staff: 15 in normal operation, 10 in emergency operation, needed within 72 h, on site. The job covers loading and delivering raw materials and meat products. In normal operation this takes 15 people. Workaround: In emergency operation 10 people are needed. The staff pool is sufficient. Additional staff can be requested.

Henning Barsch: Roles. Fleet supervisors: 1 in normal operation, 0 in emergency operation, needed within 72 h, on site. Workaround: The fleet supervisor is not needed in emergency operation. The plant manager takes over the tasks.

Henning Barsch: Partners. Vehicle workshops: The workshop partners for the Marschkamp fleet include internal and external partners, who are responsible for servicing and maintaining the vehicles. Workaround: Internal or external repair. A short-term switch is possible.

Henning Barsch: Partners. Schlugge Lagerlogistik GmbH: What the firm covers is not described. Workaround: Moving to another partner. A short-term switch is possible.

Henning Barsch: Depends on: Vehicle fleet, Fleet staff, Fleet supervisors.
