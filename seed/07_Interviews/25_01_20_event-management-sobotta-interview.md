Franziska Dohm: Thank you for the time, Marek. First the process itself: what does it do, and where does it sit?

Marek Sobotta: The IT event management process covers monitoring and managing IT events, to ensure the continuity of IT services. It includes pre-selecting the events classed as critical for the organisation, and assessing their impact on areas such as finance, law, image and operations. It is a support process, and its process owner is the head of IT operations. The organisational unit is IT.

Marek Sobotta: Health of persons. In words: 1 h none, 2 h none, 4 h none, 8 h none, 24 h none, 72 h none, 168 h none.

Marek Sobotta: Impact health: 1 h 1, 2 h 1, 4 h 1, 8 h 1, 24 h 1, 72 h 1, 168 h 1.

Marek Sobotta: Legal. In words: 1 h none, 2 h none, 4 h none, 8 h none, 24 h none, 72 h none, 168 h none.

Marek Sobotta: Impact legal: 1 h 1, 2 h 1, 4 h 1, 8 h 1, 24 h 1, 72 h 1, 168 h 1.

Franziska Dohm: How high is the potential threat, and what do you set for recovery?

Marek Sobotta: The potential threat is none. IT event management has an RTO of more than 168 hours, because financial and operational impacts of an outage are to be expected. Carrying out emergency measures, temporary solutions or the restoration of IT services can cause noticeable financial strain. Operational impacts are to be expected as well, because delays can occur in delivering important services.
