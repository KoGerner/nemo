# Raw material procurement BIA interview

**Date:** 13 February 2025, 09:45

Franziska Dohm: Thank you for the time, Jens. First the process itself: what does it do, and where does it sit?

Jens Harder: The process begins with the buyers ordering raw materials and meat products, based on current stock and on the demand of the retail locations. The buyers choose suitable suppliers and place the orders, choosing by set criteria to ensure quality and reliability. The suppliers are responsible for transporting the ordered raw materials and meat products, and they keep the cold chain while doing so. The process ends with delivery at the ramp of the production and processing site concerned, where the goods are received. It is a core process, and its process owner is purchasing management. It has no upstream process; the downstream process is production and processing.

Franziska Dohm: How high is the potential threat, and what do you set for recovery?

Jens Harder: The potential threat is high. Raw material procurement has an RTO (recovery time objective) of 72 hours, because a standstill of the process beyond that time has considerable operational, financial, legal and image impacts. If the process stands still for longer than 72 hours, the operational impacts are considerable, because individual delivery areas can be affected. Financial impacts occur with a standstill of 24 hours or longer, because overtime has to be worked, which causes costs. Legal consequences can arise if the goods are delivered late and this can no longer be worked off or made up organisationally. The company's image is badly harmed, because across regions there is no more meat in the retail locations.

Jens Harder: RTO: 72 h.

Jens Harder: MTPD: >168 h.

Jens Harder: The process is relevant to the BCMS.

Franziska Dohm: What could make the process fail?

Jens Harder: Failure of the purchasing team; supplier failure or a considerable delivery delay; interruption of transport routes or of the cold chain; failure of Microsoft 365 or Microsoft SharePoint Online; quality, veterinary or release problems with raw materials.

Franziska Dohm: And how do you run it in emergency operation?

Jens Harder: Purchasing concentrates on approved core suppliers and minimum quantities for prioritised production programmes. When systems fail, orders are placed by phone and e-mail and documented in a local order list. One buyer runs the emergency operation remotely. Delivery dates, cold-chain requirements and goods receipt are agreed directly with production and goods receipt. When suppliers fail, approved alternative suppliers are activated.
