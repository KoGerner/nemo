# Production and processing: 2026 re-interview

> Written for the demo pack, not in any prior record. This year's interview, for comparison with prior BIA record 1.2.

**Date:** Monday, 9 November 2026, 14:05
**Participants:** Henning Barsch (Plant Manager, process owner); Petra Louven (Head of Production); Dirk Wohlleben (Maintenance Manager); Marek Sobotta (Head of IT Operations); Franziska Dohm (BCM Officer, interviewer)
**Record:** re-interview of production and processing for the 2026 cycle. Last year's record is prior BIA record 1.2. Recorded in the meeting room at the Marschkamp plant.

---

Franziska Dohm: The recording is running. Thank you for making the time. This is the re-interview for production and processing. Last year's record is from September 2024. Henning, has the process itself changed?

Henning Barsch: The steps haven't. Goods come in, we check and accept them, cut, process, portion, pack, label, and it goes into chilled or intermediate storage. Three things around it have changed: a new supermarket customer, a backup for the order system, and Dirk's technicians.

Franziska Dohm: The customer first. What is new?

Henning Barsch: Since March we supply Westerkoog, a supermarket chain, every week. If we can't deliver for three days, Westerkoog cancels the contract.

Franziska Dohm: Let's take it through the impact, in words and on the scale, as last year. If production stands still, is there harm to people?

Petra Louven: No. Same as last year.

Petra Louven: Impact health: 1 h 1, 2 h 1, 4 h 1, 8 h 1, 24 h 1, 72 h 1, 168 h 1.

Franziska Dohm: Financially?

Henning Barsch: For our own stock it's as last year: we can freeze, store or sell. Westerkoog is different. After three days without deliveries we lose the contract. So high at 72 hours, and it stays high. In money: a day without production is about 0.9 million euros of goods we don't make, about 1.8 million after two days and about 2.6 million after three.

Henning Barsch: Impact financial: 1 h 1, 2 h 1, 4 h 1, 8 h 1, 24 h 1, 72 h 4, 168 h 4.

Franziska Dohm: Legal or contractual?

Henning Barsch: Yes, that is new. Westerkoog's contract has fixed delivery slots. One shift without deliveries, eight hours, and we are in breach of the delivery terms. Low from eight hours, medium after a day, high after three days, when they can cancel.

Henning Barsch: Impact legal: 1 h 1, 2 h 1, 4 h 1, 8 h 2, 24 h 3, 72 h 4, 168 h 4.

Franziska Dohm: Image?

Petra Louven: Not as last year. Inside the group others can step in, but a supermarket shelf cannot wait. From two hours it is low, after a day medium, and at three days high, because the chain will give the shelf to someone else.

Petra Louven: Impact image: 1 h 1, 2 h 2, 4 h 2, 8 h 2, 24 h 3, 72 h 4, 168 h 4.

Franziska Dohm: Operationally?

Henning Barsch: Unchanged. Up to 24 hours we make it up with extra shifts. At 72 hours it's low, we can't fully catch up. After a week it's high: real yield losses.

Henning Barsch: Impact operational: 1 h 1, 2 h 1, 4 h 1, 8 h 1, 24 h 1, 72 h 2, 168 h 4.

Franziska Dohm: Last year's record has an RTO of 72 hours and an MTPD of more than a week. Where do you stand now?

Henning Barsch: I can't say yet. That's what the BIA is for.

Franziska Dohm: What would make the process fail?

Dirk Wohlleben: A production machine down, power, refrigeration, water, compressed air.

Henning Barsch: The order system or the warehouse control going down. No raw material or packaging. Not enough people in production, cutting, engineering or cleaning.

Petra Louven: And a quality or hygiene block on an area.

Franziska Dohm: Emergency operation. Who do you need at a minimum?

Henning Barsch: As before: we cut the programme to prioritised articles and the lines that run, and we freeze or sell raw material on in a controlled way. 25 in production, 25 in cutting, one supervisor, and the technicians.

Franziska Dohm: Dirk, last year's record says four technicians in emergency operation.

Dirk Wohlleben: Four is too few. A line fault and a refrigeration fault at the same time, and four can't cover it. We need six. At night and at weekends we have two on site.

Franziska Dohm: Last year the order system, CSB, had no backup.

Marek Sobotta: That has changed. My team set up a backup order system, a second copy of CSB, at our plant in Breitenburg. If the main one goes down, we switch over to the backup.

Franziska Dohm: Has anyone tried switching over?

Marek Sobotta: No. Nobody has tried it yet.

Franziska Dohm: MAWI in Breitenburg and the warehouse control?

Henning Barsch: As last year: no workaround.

Franziska Dohm: Price labelling?

Petra Louven: As last year. Labelling needs the data from the merchandise management system, and the devices have no direct workaround.

Franziska Dohm: Refrigeration?

Dirk Wohlleben: As last year: three ranges, deep-freeze, 4 degrees and 10 degrees. If refrigeration fails, we hold the deep-freeze and clear the 4 and 10 degree areas.

Franziska Dohm: Staff numbers?

Petra Louven: The same. Cutting 40 normal, 25 in emergency. Production 40 and 25. Supervisors three, one in emergency.

Franziska Dohm: External partners?

Henning Barsch: Cleaning is Reinigung & Clar and Fresh & Clean, and there are others.

Dirk Wohlleben: External technicians are Wirfixens and Technobros. Nitrogen comes from Elbe Industrial Gases; there are other suppliers and we can switch at short notice.

Franziska Dohm: To sum up, four things changed. One: IT set up a backup order system at our Breitenburg plant, and nobody has tried switching over. Two: emergency operation needs six technicians, not four, and at night and at weekends we have only two. Three: Westerkoog cancels the contract if we can't deliver for three days. Four: Westerkoog adds legal impact from 8 hours and image impact from 2 hours. Everything else is as last year. The recovery time and the longest tolerable outage stay open until the analysis. Thank you.
